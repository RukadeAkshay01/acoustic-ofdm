Acoustic OFDM Live Link

Type anything. It becomes bits, then OFDM sound in the 4–12 kHz band, leaves the speaker, enters a microphone, and is synchronised, equalised and decoded — in front of you. The receiver is told nothing in advance; it reads the message length from the sound.

Compose and transmit

Loopback is the Phase 1/2 demo: one laptop talks to itself. Send from this device is the two-device test: open this same page on a phone or second laptop on the same Wi-Fi (address at top right), type there, and press it — that device plays the sound from its own speaker while the laptop running the server records and decodes. Listen only records the laptop mic for a few seconds so you can play a downloaded WAV from anything.

Stage-1 self-calibration (loopback only)

Sets the microphone gain, then sweeps a chirp through the speaker to find which subcarriers this laptop can actually hear. Only affects the loopback mode — a second device has its own response, so cross-device transmissions always use the full band and rely on FEC.

Not calibrated — loopback will use the full 4–12 kHz band.

Decoded output

Transmitted waveform

Silence, 30 ms chirp, guard, then the OFDM symbols. This is what the speaker plays.

Received waveform

Raw microphone capture. The frame start found by the synchroniser is marked.

Synchronisation: chirp correlation

Matched-filter output. One sharp peak means the receiver knows exactly where the frame begins.

Received spectrum

FFT of the captured frame. The 4–12 kHz block is the OFDM signal; everything else is room and mic noise.

Channel estimate |H| per subcarrier

Learned from the reference symbol and pilots: how much speaker + room + mic attenuated each frequency.

Constellation before and after equalisation

Each dot is one QPSK symbol. Four tight clouds after equalisation means clean bits.

Tracked phase drift

Common phase error per OFDM symbol. A steady slope is the signature of two devices with different sampling clocks.
